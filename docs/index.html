quant-lab

Three core quant-finance techniques, implemented from scratch in Python and originally run on historical market data, with old outputs withdrawn pending a corrected rerun: Monte Carlo portfolio risk, cointegration-based pairs trading, and a moving-average crossover backtest with honest accounting for lookahead bias and transaction costs.

02

Pairs trading / statistical arbitrage

cointegration

The earlier model fitted log prices but traded a level-price spread, so its published metrics are withdrawn. The corrected implementation tests log-price cointegration, uses a matching log spread, disjoint windows and gross-normalized illustrative returns. Failed screens stop the run. It still excludes borrowing, financing and execution frictions.

03

50/200 moving average crossover

backtest

The golden-cross rule on SPY over 15 years: hold while the 50-day average is above the 200-day, otherwise sit in cash. Signals are delayed one day to remove lookahead bias, every switch pays 10 bps, and the benchmark is plain buy-and-hold. The earlier result is withdrawn until initial-peak drawdown and the full run are rechecked.

Honest limits. The rule spends most of its life in the market and exits late in fast crashes, so it lags buy-and-hold in a long bull run. It is an educational baseline, not a recommendation.
